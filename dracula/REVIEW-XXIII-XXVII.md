# Dracula, Chapters XXIII–XXVII — speech attributions

Each document is read by its writer; a principal character inside a journal, diary or memorandum speaks for himself or herself — the Count at Piccadilly (XXIII), and the fair woman of the castle calling “Come, sister” (XXVII); anyone else — the skipper of the Czarina Catherine, the crowd at Galatz — is spoken by the document’s writer. Mina’s hypnotic answers are hers. A quoted word or report (“Not yet reported.”, “action.”) is the writer’s. The telegrams of XXV are read whole by Stoker. Every attribution not from an explicit tag was read and set by hand: **confirmed** where the automatic pass had it right, **corrected** where it did not.
