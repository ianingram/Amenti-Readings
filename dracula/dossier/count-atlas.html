The Count’s Atlas

“One was an atlas, which I found opened naturally at England, as if that map had been much used.” The page as Jonathan saw it, and the estate he sold the Count, drawn from his own notes.

The rings and the words are Stoker’s; the atlas page is drawn on modern terrain and coasts (Natural Earth), coloured like an atlas of the day. Carfax is Stoker’s invention and he gives no plan of it: this one is drawn only from what Jonathan wrote — the by-road, the high old wall and its rusted gates, the four-sided house set to the points of the compass, some twenty acres of trees, the dark pond and its stream, the stone keep and the old chapel, and the asylum beyond the wall. Everything else on it is a guess, and the shapes are illustration.
