The Count’s History

“Whenever he spoke of his house he always said ‘we’ … It seemed to have in it a whole history of the country.” The peoples and battles of the Count’s speech, as he names them.

a people on the move a guarded frontier a battle — click for pictures

The words are the Count’s, as Jonathan Harker set them down; the dates are added. Some of it is his boast rather than history — the Szekelys’ descent from Attila’s Huns was their own legend, and the “Ugric tribe from Iceland” is Stoker’s. The “Voivode” who “crossed the Danube and beat the Turk on his own ground” is Vlad III Dracula, whose winter campaign of 1462 ran along the Danube to the sea.
